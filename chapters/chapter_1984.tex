for his development of methodology for chemical synthesis on a solid matrix

\section{Robert Bruce Merrifield}

\subsection*{Early Life and Education}

Robert Bruce Merrifield was born on 1921-07-15 in Fort Worth, Texas, USA.

\subsection*{Career and Major Research}

Merrifield studied at the University of California, Los Angeles, where he received his doctorate in 1949. He joined the Rockefeller Institute in New York, where he developed solid-phase synthesis, a method for building peptides step by step on insoluble polymer beads.

\subsection*{Nobel Prize-winning Work}

The work for which Robert Bruce Merrifield was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1984 was described by the Nobel Committee as: "for his development of methodology for chemical synthesis on a solid matrix".

In solid-phase peptide synthesis, amino acids are attached to a solid support and coupled one at a time with washing in between, making peptide assembly rapid and amenable to automation.

\subsection*{Significance and Impact}

The method made it practical to synthesize peptides and proteins, including insulin and enzymes, and became the basis of automated peptide synthesizers.

\subsection*{Later Career and Honors}

Merrifield remained at Rockefeller University until his retirement. He died on 2006-05-14 in Cresskill, New Jersey, USA.

\subsection*{Legacy}

Solid-phase synthesis is the foundation of modern peptide chemistry and peptide-based drugs.

\subsection*{Selected Publications}

"Solid Phase Peptide Synthesis. I. The Synthesis of a Tetrapeptide" (Journal of the American Chemical Society, 1963)

\section*{Chapter Summary}

The 1984 prize honored Robert Bruce Merrifield for developing methodology for chemical synthesis on a solid matrix. His solid-phase peptide synthesis transformed the production of peptides and proteins.
