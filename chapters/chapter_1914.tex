\section{Theodore W. Richards}

\subsection*{Early Life and Education}

Theodore W. Richards was born on 1868-01-31 in Germantown, PA, USA.

\subsection*{Career and Major Research}

Richards was professor of chemistry at Harvard, where he spent his entire career. He refined the determinations of atomic weights of more than sixty elements, correcting many values, and his measurements of the atomic weight of lead established that the values varied with the source of the lead, a fact explained by isotopes. He was the first American chemist to receive the prize.

\subsection*{Nobel Prize-winning Work}

The work for which Theodore W. Richards was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1914 was described by the Nobel Committee as: "in recognition of his accurate determinations of the atomic weight of a large number of chemical elements".

This research fundamentally advanced the field by making groundbreaking contributions that transformed chemical science.

\subsection*{Significance and Impact}

The impact of Theodore W. Richards's work extends far beyond the specific achievement recognized by the Nobel Prize.
These contributions continue to influence chemical research and education today.

\subsection*{Later Career and Honors}

Theodore W. Richards continued his scientific work until 1928-04-02, passing away in Cambridge, MA, USA.
He received numerous honors including membership in major scientific academies and societies.

\subsection*{Legacy}

The legacy of Theodore W. Richards endures through the lasting impact of his scientific contributions.
Theodore W. Richards's work continues to be cited and built upon by researchers across the chemical sciences.

\subsection*{Modern Developments}

Richards's precise determinations of atomic weights established rigorous analytical chemistry and laid the groundwork for the modern periodic table and the discovery of isotopes. His mass-based methods evolved into modern mass spectrometry and isotopic analysis used in radiometric dating, forensic science, and the calibration of the atomic mass unit.

\subsection*{Selected Publications}

Theodore W. Richards's major publications include numerous papers in leading journals of the era, detailing the experimental and theoretical work summarized above.
