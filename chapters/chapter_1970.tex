\section{Luis Leloir}

\subsection*{Early Life and Education}

Luis Leloir was born on 1906-09-06 in Paris, France.

\subsection*{Career and Major Research}

Leloir studied medicine at the University of Buenos Aires, where he later worked as a researcher. He established an institute in Buenos Aires, later named the Leloir Institute, and studied the metabolism of carbohydrates. He discovered uridine diphosphate glucose and other sugar nucleotides and showed that they act as intermediates in the biosynthesis of glycogen and other carbohydrates.

\subsection*{Nobel Prize-winning Work}

The work for which Luis Leloir was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1970 was described by the Nobel Committee as: "for his discovery of sugar nucleotides and their role in the biosynthesis of carbohydrates".

Leloir found that sugars must be activated by attachment to nucleotides such as uridine diphosphate before they can be incorporated into polysaccharides, revealing the general mechanism of carbohydrate biosynthesis.

\subsection*{Significance and Impact}

The discovery of sugar nucleotides explained how glycogen, starch, and other carbohydrates are synthesized in cells and opened the field of glycobiology.

\subsection*{Later Career and Honors}

Leloir continued to direct his institute in Buenos Aires until his death. He died on 1987-12-02 in Buenos Aires, Argentina.

\subsection*{Legacy}

Sugar nucleotides are recognized as fundamental building blocks of carbohydrate metabolism in all organisms.

\section*{Chapter Summary}

The 1970 prize honored Luis Leloir for the discovery of sugar nucleotides and their role in carbohydrate biosynthesis. His work revealed the molecular mechanism by which cells build polysaccharides.
