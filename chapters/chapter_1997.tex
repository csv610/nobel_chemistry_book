\chapter{1997:Paul D. Boyer and John E. Walker and Jens C. Skou}

\label{chap:chemistry1997}

The Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the year 1997 was awarded jointly to Paul D. Boyer and John E. Walker and Jens C. Skou.

\textbf{Motivation:}

for their elucidation of the enzymatic mechanism underlying the synthesis of adenosine triphosphate (ATP)

\section{Paul D. Boyer}

\subsection*{Early Life and Education}

Paul D. Boyer was born in 1918 in Provo, Utah, USA.

\subsection*{Career and Major Research}

Boyer studied at Brigham Young University (B.S., 1939) and earned his Ph.D. from the University of Wisconsin in 1943. He joined the University of California, Los Angeles, in 1963, where he worked until his retirement.

\subsection*{Nobel Prize-winning Work}

The work for which Paul D. Boyer was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1997 was described by the Nobel Committee as: "for their elucidation of the enzymatic mechanism underlying the synthesis of adenosine triphosphate (ATP)".

He proposed the binding-change mechanism of ATP synthase, in which the energy of a proton gradient drives the release of ATP from the enzyme rather than its formation directly.

\subsection*{Significance and Impact}

The mechanism explained how ATP synthase couples proton flow to ATP synthesis in all living cells.

\subsection*{Later Career and Honors}

Boyer was professor emeritus at the University of California, Los Angeles. He shared the 1997 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, and he died in 2018.

\subsection*{Legacy}

The binding-change mechanism is now a central concept in bioenergetics.

\subsection*{Selected Publications}

"The Binding Change Mechanism for ATP Synthase: Some Probabilities and Possibilities" (Biochimica et Biophysica Acta, 1993)

\clearpage

\section{John E. Walker}

\subsection*{Early Life and Education}

John E. Walker was born in 1941 in Halifax, England.

\subsection*{Career and Major Research}

Walker studied at the University of Oxford (B.A., 1964; Ph.D., 1969). He joined the Medical Research Council Laboratory of Molecular Biology in Cambridge in 1974, where he spent his career.

\subsection*{Nobel Prize-winning Work}

The work for which John E. Walker was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1997 was described by the Nobel Committee as: "for their elucidation of the enzymatic mechanism underlying the synthesis of adenosine triphosphate (ATP)".

He determined the amino acid sequences and the three-dimensional structure of the catalytic F1 part of ATP synthase, confirming the rotary binding-change mechanism.

\subsection*{Significance and Impact}

The atomic structure provided direct evidence for how the enzyme synthesizes ATP by rotation.

\subsection*{Later Career and Honors}

Walker continued at the Medical Research Council Laboratory of Molecular Biology in Cambridge. He shared the 1997 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

\subsection*{Legacy}

His structure of F1-ATPase remains a landmark in molecular biology and bioenergetics.

\subsection*{Selected Publications}

"Structure at 2.8 Å Resolution of F1-ATPase from Bovine Heart Mitochondria" (Nature, 1994)

\clearpage

\section{Jens C. Skou}

\subsection*{Early Life and Education}

Jens C. Skou was born in 1918 in Lemvig, Denmark.

\subsection*{Career and Major Research}

Skou earned his M.D. from the University of Copenhagen in 1944. He was a professor of biophysics at the University of Aarhus.

\subsection*{Nobel Prize-winning Work}

The work for which Jens C. Skou was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1997 was described by the Nobel Committee as: "for their elucidation of the enzymatic mechanism underlying the synthesis of adenosine triphosphate (ATP)".

He discovered the sodium-potassium ATPase, the enzyme that pumps sodium and potassium ions across cell membranes using energy from ATP.

\subsection*{Significance and Impact}

This was the first known ion pump, and it is essential for nerve and muscle function.

\subsection*{Later Career and Honors}

Skou remained at the University of Aarhus. He shared the 1997 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, and he died in 2018.

\subsection*{Legacy}

His discovery founded the study of transport ATPases, a major family of membrane proteins.

\subsection*{Selected Publications}

"The Influence of Some Cations on an Adenosine Triphosphatase from Peripheral Nerves" (Biochimica et Biophysica Acta, 1957)

\clearpage

\section*{Chapter Summary}

The 1997 Nobel Prize in Chemistry recognized the enzymes that synthesize ATP and use its energy. Boyer and Walker explained the mechanism of ATP synthase, while Skou discovered the sodium-potassium pump.
