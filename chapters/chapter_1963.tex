\chapter{1963:Karl Ziegler and Giulio Natta}

\label{chap:chemistry1963}

The Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the year 1963 was awarded jointly to Karl Ziegler and Giulio Natta.

\textbf{Motivation:}

for their discoveries in the field of the chemistry and technology of high polymers

\section{Karl Ziegler}

\subsection*{Early Life and Education}

Karl Ziegler was born on 1898-11-26 in Helsa, Germany.

\subsection*{Career and Major Research}

Ziegler studied chemistry at the universities of Marburg and Munich and received his doctorate from the University of Marburg in 1920. He became director of the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute, later the Max Planck Institute, for Coal Research in M{\"u}lheim an der Ruhr, where he spent most of his career. His research on organometallic compounds, especially the aluminium alkyls, led in 1953 to the discovery that combinations of an aluminium alkyl with a titanium compound could polymerize ethylene at ordinary pressure to linear polyethylene.

\subsection*{Nobel Prize-winning Work}

The work for which Karl Ziegler was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1963 was described by the Nobel Committee as: "for their discoveries in the field of the chemistry and technology of high polymers".

Ziegler's organometallic catalysts polymerized ethylene at low pressure to a linear, high-density polyethylene, in contrast to the high-pressure radical process then in use. The discovery provided a general catalytic route to polyolefins and became the basis of an entire industrial technology.

\subsection*{Significance and Impact}

The low-pressure polymerization of ethylene transformed the plastics industry, making polyethylene cheaper and giving it new properties. The catalyst systems that Ziegler discovered, later combined with Natta's stereospecific polymers as Ziegler-Natta catalysts, produce the majority of the world's polyethylene and polypropylene.

\subsection*{Later Career and Honors}

Ziegler remained director of the Max Planck Institute for Coal Research in M{\"u}lheim until 1969. He died on 1973-08-11 in M{\"u}lheim, Germany.

\subsection*{Legacy}

Ziegler's work founded the chemistry of organoaluminium polymerization catalysts, and the Ziegler-Natta catalysts that bear his name dominate the industrial production of polyolefin plastics.

\subsection*{Modern Developments}

Ziegler's discovery of organometallic catalysts for ethylene polymerization created the foundation of the modern plastics industry and of organometallic catalysis. Ziegler–Natta catalysts produce polyethylene and polypropylene, among the most widely used materials on Earth. Ziegler's organoaluminum chemistry also underpins industrial aluminum alkyl synthesis and modern polymer science.

\section{Giulio Natta}

\subsection*{Early Life and Education}

Giulio Natta was born on 1903-02-26 in Imperia, Italy.

\subsection*{Career and Major Research}

Natta studied chemical engineering at the Polytechnic of Milan, where he received his doctorate in 1924. He held professorships at the universities of Pavia, Rome, and Turin before returning to the Polytechnic of Milan in 1938. Learning of Ziegler's catalysts, he applied them to the polymerization of propylene and in 1954 produced isotactic polypropylene, in which the methyl groups are arranged regularly along the chain, giving a crystalline, strong plastic.

\subsection*{Nobel Prize-winning Work}

The work for which Giulio Natta was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1963 was described by the Nobel Committee as: "for their discoveries in the field of the chemistry and technology of high polymers".

Natta showed that Ziegler-type catalysts could control the spatial arrangement of monomer units during polymerization, producing stereospecific polymers with regular structure. His discovery of isotactic polypropylene established stereospecific polymerization and the field of polymer stereochemistry.

\subsection*{Significance and Impact}

Isotactic polypropylene became one of the world's most important plastics, and Natta's concept of stereospecific polymerization explained how the regular arrangement of groups along a polymer chain determines its crystallinity and properties. He introduced the terms isotactic, syndiotactic, and atactic.

\subsection*{Later Career and Honors}

Natta remained professor at the Polytechnic of Milan and continued research in polymer chemistry until ill health forced his retirement in 1963. He died on 1979-05-02 in Bergamo, Italy.

\subsection*{Legacy}

Natta is regarded as the founder of stereospecific polymerization, and the Ziegler-Natta catalysts are among the most important catalysts in industrial chemistry, producing polypropylene and polyethylene on a massive scale.

\subsection*{Modern Developments}

Natta's stereospecific polymerization using Ziegler–Natta catalysts produced the first isotactic polypropylene, founding the modern stereoregular polymer industry. Polypropylene is now one of the world's most produced plastics, used in packaging, textiles, and medical products. Natta's work pioneered the study of polymer tacticity and its control of material properties.

\section*{Chapter Summary}

The 1963 prize recognized discoveries in the chemistry and technology of high polymers. Karl Ziegler discovered organoaluminium-titanium catalysts that polymerize ethylene at low pressure, and Giulio Natta applied them to produce stereospecific polymers such as isotactic polypropylene, founding the industrial polyolefin technology.
