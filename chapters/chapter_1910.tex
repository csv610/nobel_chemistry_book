\section{Otto Wallach}

\subsection*{Early Life and Education}

Otto Wallach was born on 1847-03-27 in Koenigsberg, Germany (now Kaliningrad, Russia).
Otto Wallach was a German chemist and recipient of the 1910 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his work on alicyclic compounds.

\subsection*{Career and Major Research}

Wallach was professor of chemistry at Göttingen from 1876 to 1915. He carried out a systematic investigation of the terpenes, the volatile constituents of essential oils, determining their structures and establishing the isoprene rule. His work transformed the chemistry of terpenes and provided a basis for the synthetic perfume industry.

\subsection*{Nobel Prize-winning Work}

The work for which Otto Wallach was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1910 was described by the Nobel Committee as: "in recognition of his services to organic chemistry and the chemical industry by his pioneer work in the field of alicyclic compounds".

This research fundamentally advanced the field by making groundbreaking contributions that transformed chemical science.

\subsection*{Significance and Impact}

The impact of Otto Wallach's work extends far beyond the specific achievement recognized by the Nobel Prize.
These contributions continue to influence chemical research and education today.

\subsection*{Later Career and Honors}

Otto Wallach continued his scientific work until 1931-02-26, passing away in Göttingen, Germany.
He received numerous honors including membership in major scientific academies and societies.

\subsection*{Legacy}

The legacy of Otto Wallach endures through the lasting impact of his scientific contributions.
Otto Wallach's work continues to be cited and built upon by researchers across the chemical sciences.

\subsection*{Selected Publications}

Otto Wallach's major publications include numerous papers in leading journals of the era, detailing the experimental and theoretical work summarized above.
